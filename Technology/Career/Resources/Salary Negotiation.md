# Salary Negotiation

## Mindset for Interviews and Salary Negotiation

- **It is not "asking for a job"**: It's a fair exchange - you rent out your skills and receive salary, opportunities, and benefits in return
- **Salary is a bundle of value**:
  - The monthly amount you receive
  - Opportunities to learn and grow professionally
  - Opportunities to develop your abilities (onsite, international colleagues, workshops, technical support)
  - The career advancement path
  - Finding good mentors and good friends
  - Company culture
  - The chance to stay for the long haul

## Salary Is the Last Topic

- Steer to another topic when asked about salary
- "Salary is the last thing I'll discuss" - after everything else is cleared up
- Employers appreciate this

## Don't Name a Number First

- Whoever names a number first loses negotiating leverage
- Steer toward technical matters and ask for their number
- **If you're forced to**: Give a range, starting from your expectation
- **Example**: "For this position and my abilities, it would be in the range of 30-37 million VND"
- **Key phrase**: "offers from a few other companies" - which means you need to interview at many places

## Negotiation Starts When You Have an Offer

- All the earlier interviews set the stage for the salary negotiation
- **If the offer is low**: Accept a probation period and renegotiate after 2-6 months, or take salary + commission/equity
- **If the offer is higher than expected**: You can still keep negotiating upward
- **Guiding principle**: If you ask, you may not get it; if you don't ask, you definitely won't

## Understand the Rules of the Game

- The salary decision-maker may be the interviewer, a superior, HR, or a committee
- **Usual process**: Technical interview → scoring → propose the maximum level → HR writes up a justification to bring it down
- **Try to close the offer during the interview**: Be proactive
- **If negotiating by phone**: "This offer is quite attractive; I'll get back to you within 3 days at most"
- **Meeting a C-level**: Negotiation is easier, and they close on the spot

## The Interview

- **CV**: No typos; show 3 things: what you did, what you learned, and the difficulties you faced and how you solved them
- **Prepare questions**: Expertise, policies, culture, people, direction
- **Punctuality and demeanor**: Discipline matters a lot
- **Say only what you know**: Don't bluff; if you don't know, say so honestly

## The Core

- You must have real ability, exactly what the company needs, and show it to them
