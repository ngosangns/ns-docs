# Growth Management

Growth management (the discipline behind "growth hacking") is a system of running rapid, measurable experiments across the whole funnel to grow a business metric — not a marketing tactic. Sean Ellis coined "growth hacker" in 2010 for people whose only goal is growth ("a growth hacker is not a marketing Director... their true north is growth").

## History and core test

- **Sean Ellis (2010)** — coined growth hacking while at Dropbox; later founded GrowthHackers.
- **Product-market fit test** — his survey question: "How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]?" **≥ 40% answering "very disappointed"** signals PMF. Below that, growth tactics amplify a leaky bucket.
- The discipline matured from hacks into a structured system: data → ideas → prioritized experiments → analysis → repeat.

## AARRR vs growth loops

**AARRR "pirate metrics"** (Dave McClure) — the classic funnel: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue. Funnels are linear: customers enter, some convert, the rest leak out.

**Growth loops** (Brian Balfour / Reforge) — the newer model. A loop is a closed system where the output of one cycle is reinvested as input to the next:

```
Investment → Action → Output → (reinvested) → Action → ...
```

Loop types:

- **Usage loops** — using the product creates more value or inventory (e.g. user-generated content attracting more users).
- **Viral loops** — one user's usage exposes new users (invites, shared docs, social feeds).
- **Paid loops** — revenue is reinvested into acquisition (CAC < LTV).
- **Sales loops** — sales output funds more sales capacity and targeting data.
- **Ecosystem loops** — partners/plugins extend the product (platform effects).

Implication: model your growth as the loop you can compound, then measure each step of it.

## North Star and input metrics

- **North Star Metric (NSM)** — one metric that best captures the value delivered to customers (e.g. Airbnb nights booked, Spotify time listening). Not revenue itself — a proxy for delivered value that correlates with it.
- **Input metrics** — the levers under the NSM (signups, activation rate, retention cohorts). Teams own input metrics; the NSM keeps them aligned. The tree of NSM → input metrics is the growth equivalent of an opportunity solution tree.

## Experimentation

- **High-tempo testing** — Sean Ellis' framework: 1-2-3 in the first 4 weeks (1 big idea, 2 must-win tests, 3 days per test cycle) to find the first win fast.
- Standard cadence: ICE-prioritized experiment queue → ship → measure → document → re-rank. Expect most experiments to fail; the value is in the compounding wins.
- **Vitamins vs painkillers** — growth experiments that only add surface polish ("vitamin") rarely move metrics; experiments that remove real friction ("painkiller") do.

## Team structure

- **Growth team** — a cross-functional pod: PM (or growth lead), data analyst, engineer(s), designer, sometimes a marketer. Often owns its own backlog and even its own engineering pod.
- **Where it sits** — independent team ("growth squad") targeting the funnel end-to-end, or embedded with product teams. Reed Hastings' famous framing (Growbot at Facebook): growth as a dedicated machine.
- **Growth vs marketing** — marketing owns awareness and demand channels at the top; growth owns the entire funnel and is accountable to a metric, running product-led experiments, not just campaigns.

## When to invest

- Growth work **after product-market fit** — the 40% test above. Pre-PMF, effort goes to retention and product.
- **PLG (product-led growth)** — when the product itself is the main acquisition/activation channel (Slack, Figma, Dropbox); growth loops and PLG usually go together.

## Growth PM vs core PM

A **Growth PM** runs short-horizon (weeks) experiments on a business metric anywhere in the funnel, often on a dedicated growth pod. A **core PM** owns long-term product strategy for a surface. Companies differ on whether growth is a separate pod or a partner discipline to the core PM. Details: [Product Management](/Technology/Career/Concepts/Product Management).
