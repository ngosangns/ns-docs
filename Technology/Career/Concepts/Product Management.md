# Product Management

Product management sits at the intersection of business, technology, and user experience. Marty Cagan's framing: a PM is responsible for **discovering a product that is valuable, usable, feasible, and viable** — the PM owns the "why" and "what"; engineering owns the "how"; design owns the experience.

## What a PM actually does

- **Discovery** — talking to users, running interviews and tests to find problems worth solving (Teresa Torres' continuous discovery: weekly touchpoints with users).
- **Delivery** — writing specs/PRDs, working with engineering and design through build and launch, handling trade-offs.
- **Strategy** — owning the product vision (where the product is going) and translating it into a roadmap, which is a communication artifact of strategy, not a fixed feature list.
- **Prioritization** — deciding what NOT to build; sequencing work against goals and constraints.
- **Stakeholder alignment** — keeping leadership, sales, marketing, support, and engineering pointed at the same outcomes.
- **Metrics** — defining success measures and being accountable for outcomes, not output (shipping features is output; moving a metric is outcome).

## Prioritization frameworks

- **RICE** — (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort; good for expected-value ranking.
- **ICE** — Impact × Confidence × Ease; a lighter version for faster scoring.
- **Kano** — classifies features as basic, performance, or delighter; useful for customer satisfaction trade-offs.
- **Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)** — frame demand around the "job" a user hires the product for, not personas or features.
- **Opportunity Solution Trees** (Teresa Torres) — map business outcome → opportunities → solutions → experiments, keeping discovery tied to a measurable outcome.

## PM vs related roles

| Role                            | Owns                                                                                        | Analogy (Evelyn Hartz)                                                 |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Product Manager                 | What gets built and why: vision, strategy, roadmap, discovery                               | The cereal is ON the shelf                                             |
| Product Marketing Manager (PMM) | Go-to-market: positioning, messaging, launch, sales enablement                              | The cereal is OFF the shelf                                            |
| Product Owner                   | Scrum-team-level backlog executor; in many orgs the same person as PM with a narrower scope | —                                                                      |
| Growth PM                       | Short-horizon experiments on a business metric anywhere in the funnel                       | See [Growth Management](/Technology/Career/Concepts/Growth Management) |

Brett Byron's analogy: PM and PMM are driver and navigator — PM drives product direction, PMM navigates positioning and messaging; neither works without the other.

A common myth: "PM is the CEO of the product." Cagan and others push back — PMs have responsibility without authority and must lead through influence.

## Career ladder (typical big-tech shape)

1. **APM** — executes scoped features with guidance.
2. **PM** — owns a product area end to end.
3. **Senior PM** — owns a complex area, mentors, handles ambiguity.
4. **Principal / Group PM** — owns a product group or leads PMs on one large surface.
5. **Director → VP → CPO** — owns a portfolio, org design, and the connection between product strategy and company strategy.

## How the job changes by company stage

- **Startup (pre-PMF)** — everything is discovery; PM wears GTM, support, and analytics hats.
- **Scale-up** — process, prioritization, and stakeholder management dominate; roadmaps matter.
- **Big tech** — deep specialization (platform PM, growth PM, API PM), heavy cross-functional coordination.
