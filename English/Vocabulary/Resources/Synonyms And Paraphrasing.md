# Synonyms And Paraphrasing

Paraphrasing — saying the same idea in different words — is the core exam skill in reading (recognizing reworded answers) and writing (avoiding repetition).

## Paraphrase Techniques

1. **Swap synonyms:** _important → crucial_, _buy → purchase_.
2. **Change word form:** _He decided to go → He made a decision to go_ (verb → noun) — see [Word Formation](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Word Formation).
3. **Change voice:** _They built the bridge in 1990 → The bridge was built in 1990_ — see [Passive Voice](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Passive Voice).
4. **Change sentence structure:** merge/split clauses, reorder — _Because it rained, we stayed home → We stayed home because of the rain_ — see [Sentence Transformation Patterns](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Sentence Transformation Patterns).
5. **Generalize/specify:** _dogs → pets → animals_; _smartphones → electronic devices_.

## Reporting Verbs by Stance

For citing ideas in essays — pick the verb that matches the author's certainty:

- **Neutral:** say, state, note, mention, report, describe.
- **Agreement/support:** argue, claim, suggest, point out, emphasize, stress.
- **Doubt/criticism:** question, doubt, criticize, challenge, warn.

## Notes

- Synonyms are rarely perfect — check register ([Formal And Informal English](/English/Usage/Concepts/Formal And Informal English)) and collocation ([Collocations](/English/Vocabulary/Concepts/Collocations)) before swapping: _make a decision_ not "do a decision"; _strong coffee_ not "powerful coffee".
- In paraphrase questions, the correct option keeps the **meaning** — not the fanciest words.
