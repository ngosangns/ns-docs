# Collocations

A collocation is a **natural word combination** — "make a decision", not "do a decision". Collocations are largely arbitrary and must be learned as chunks; they are the difference between correct and natural English.

## Verb + Noun

| Verb     | Collocations                                                                        |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| make     | a decision, a mistake, an effort, money, friends, a suggestion, progress, an excuse |
| do       | homework, business, a favour, damage, research, the dishes, your best               |
| take     | a break, a chance, a photo, notes, care, medicine, a risk, an exam                  |
| have     | breakfast, fun, a rest, a party, a problem, experience, a word with sb              |
| get      | married, divorced, fired, lost, ready, a job, permission                            |
| catch    | a bus, a cold, a thief, someone's attention, fire                                   |
| break    | a record, the law, a promise, the news, someone's heart, a habit                    |
| keep     | a promise, a secret, calm, in touch, quiet, the change                              |
| save     | time, money, energy, someone's life, a seat                                         |
| pay      | attention, a visit, a bill, a compliment, respect                                   |
| miss     | a bus, a chance, a class, a deadline                                                |
| win/lose | a game, a prize, a match, weight, one's temper                                      |

**Make vs Do** — the top exam trap: "make" creates/produces a result; "do" performs an activity. _make dinner, make friends_ but _do homework, do exercise, do the shopping._

## Adjective + Noun

- heavy rain, heavy smoker, heavy traffic, heavy drinker
- strong coffee/tea, strong accent, strong point (NOT "powerful coffee")
- fast car, fast food (BUT quick meal, quick lunch)
- high/low price, high priority, high quality
- wide range, wide experience, broad smile, broad daylight
- big mistake, great pleasure, great deal, golden opportunity
- narrow escape, slim chance, tight schedule, sore throat

## Adverb + Adjective

- deeply sorry, deeply concerned, deeply religious
- highly unlikely, highly recommended, highly qualified
- perfectly clear, perfectly normal
- bitterly disappointed, bitterly cold
- utterly ridiculous, absolutely amazing, completely wrong
- seriously ill, critically important, painfully slow

## Verb + Adverb

- work hard, study hard, rain heavily, snow heavily
- sleep soundly, apologize sincerely, thank warmly
- drive carefully, listen attentively, think seriously
- fail miserably, forget completely, remember clearly/vividly

## Noun + Verb and Other Patterns

- time flies, prices rise, alarms go off, plans change
- adjective + preposition — see the dependent-preposition lists in [Prepositions](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Prepositions)
- verb + preposition: depend on, listen to, apply for — same link

## Notes

- When a combination "translates correctly" but sounds wrong, it's usually a collocation issue — check a collocation dictionary (e.g., OZDIC).
- Learn vocabulary in chunks: record "commit a crime" not just "commit".
- In exams, collocations drive cloze-test and word-choice answers — especially make/do, heavy/strong, say/tell.
