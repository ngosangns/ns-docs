# Participle Clauses

A participle clause uses a participle (V-ing or V3/ed) to compress information into the sentence — a shorter alternative to a full clause.

## Present Participle (V-ing) — Active Meaning

Replaces an active clause where the subject performs the action:

- _Walking down the street, I met an old friend._ (= While I was walking...) (Đang đi dạo, tôi gặp một người bạn cũ.)
- _The girl sitting next to me is Lan._ (= who is sitting — reduced [relative clause](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Relative Clauses))
- Reason: _Feeling tired, she went to bed early._ (= Because she felt tired...)
- Result: _The fire spread quickly, destroying the whole building._

## Past Participle (V3/ed) — Passive Meaning

Replaces a passive clause:

- _Built in 1889, the tower attracts millions of visitors._ (= which was built...)
- _Asked about his plans, he refused to answer._ (= When he was asked...)
- _The books written by Hemingway are classics._

## Perfect Participle (Having + V3) — Earlier Action

Shows the participle action happened **before** the main action:

- _Having finished his homework, he went out to play._ (= After he had finished...) (Làm xong bài tập, cậu ấy ra ngoài chơi.)
- _Not having eaten all day, she was starving._
- Passive form: _Having been warned, they stayed away._

## Participle after a Conjunction

Conjunctions like **when, while, after, before, if, although, though, since, once, unless** can precede a participle to keep the time/concession/condition explicit:

- _While waiting for the bus, I read the news._ (Trong khi chờ xe buýt, tôi đọc tin tức.)
- _If asked, I will help._ (Nếu được hỏi, tôi sẽ giúp.)
- _Although tired, she kept working._ (Dù mệt, cô ấy vẫn tiếp tục làm.)
- _Once opened, the milk should be used within three days._

## Notes

- **Subject match:** the implied subject of the participle must be the subject of the main clause — "Walking down the street, the trees looked beautiful" is wrong (trees can't walk).
- Participle clauses are more common in writing and formal English than in speech.
- Choosing V-ing vs V3/ed: active meaning → V-ing; passive meaning → V3/ed — the same logic as reducing [relative clauses](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Relative Clauses).
