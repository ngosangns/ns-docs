# Phrasal Verbs

A phrasal verb is a **verb + particle** (adverb or preposition: up, on, off, in, out, over, away...) whose meaning is often idiomatic — different from the base verb: _give up_ (quit), _look after_ (care for).

## Types

### Intransitive — No Object

_The plane took off._ · _He grew up in Hanoi._ · _My car broke down._

### Separable — Object Can Split the Verb and Particle

- _Turn **off** the light_ = _Turn the light **off**._
- **Pronoun objects must split:** _Turn it off_ — never "turn off it".

Common separable: bring up, call off, carry out, fill in/out, find out, give up, look up, make up, pick up, point out, put off, put on, take off, take up, try on, turn down/off/up, work out.

### Inseparable — Object Stays After the Particle

- _Look **after** the children._ · _I ran **into** an old friend._
- Pronouns still follow: _look after them._

Common inseparable: account for, come across, deal with, get over, go over, look after/for/into, run into, stand for, take after, call on, get on (a bus).

### Three-Word Phrasal Verbs — Verb + Adverb + Preposition

Always inseparable: _put up with noise, look forward to the trip._

Common: look forward to, put up with, catch up with, get along with, come up with, run out of, look up to, look down on, keep up with, cut down on, do away with, get away with.

## Notes

- Gerund/particle trap: after preposition-particles use V-ing — _look forward to **seeing** you, give up **smoking**_ (the "to" is a preposition — see [Infinitives And Gerunds](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Infinitives And Gerunds)).
- Register: phrasal verbs are informal — formal writing often prefers one-word equivalents (put off → postpone, find out → discover, put up with → tolerate).
- Same verb + different particle = different meaning; always learn the pair, not the verb alone.
