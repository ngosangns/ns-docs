# Noun Clauses

A noun clause is a dependent clause that works **as a noun** — as subject, object, or complement. The other two dependent-clause types are [relative clauses](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Relative Clauses) and [adverbial clauses](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Adverbial Clauses).

## Starters

- **that** — for statements: _That he lied surprised everyone._
- **wh- words** — what, who, when, where, why, how, which, whose: _I know what you did._
- **if / whether** — for yes/no meaning: _I don't know if she will come._

## Functions

### As Subject

- _That he passed the exam surprised everyone._ (Việc anh ấy đậu khiến mọi người ngạc nhiên.)
- _What you said is true._ (Điều bạn nói là đúng.)
- Long subject clauses are usually moved behind "it": _It is clear that he lied._ — see [There Be And It](/English/Grammar/Concepts/There Be And It).

### As Object

- After verbs: _I believe that she is honest._ · _Do you know where he lives?_
- After "it" in structures like _find/make/think + it + adj + that/to_: _I find it strange that he left early._ — see Model 6 of [Sentence Transformation Patterns](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Sentence Transformation Patterns).

### As Complement

- After "be" and linking verbs: _The truth is that he lied._ (Sự thật là anh ấy đã nói dối.)
- _This is what I want._

## Statement Word Order

A noun clause keeps **statement order** — subject before verb — even when it begins with a question word:

- _Where does he live?_ → _I know where he lives._ (not "where does he live")
- _What happened?_ → _Tell me what happened._

## That-Clauses after Verbs and Adjectives

- **Reporting verbs:** say, tell, think, believe, know, hope, agree, admit — _He said that he was tired._ — tense backshift is covered in [Reported Speech](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Reported Speech).
- **After adjectives:** glad, sure, afraid, sorry, certain — _I am glad that you came._
- **Mandative subjunctive:** after suggest/insist/demand and "it is important that" the verb stays bare — see [Subjunctive Mood](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Subjunctive Mood).
- "That" is optional after common verbs: _I think (that) she is right._

## Whether vs If

Both introduce yes/no clauses after verbs: _I wonder whether/if he will come._ Only **whether** is used:

- after prepositions: _It depends on whether he comes._
- before "or not" directly: _whether or not he comes._
- as subject or with to-infinitive: _Whether he comes is unclear._ · _I can't decide whether to go._

## Notes

- A noun clause takes a **singular** verb when it is the subject: _What they need is more time._
- Distinguish from relative clauses: in _I know what you did_, "what" both introduces the clause and acts inside it; a relative clause modifies a noun already present.
