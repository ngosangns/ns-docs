# Nouns

A noun names a person, place, thing, or idea. For plural-formation rules, see [Singular And Plural](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Singular And Plural).

## Countable vs Uncountable

|                | Countable                         | Uncountable                                   |
| -------------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Can be counted | one book, two books               | water, information (no plural)                |
| Takes a/an     | yes                               | no                                            |
| Quantifiers    | many, a few, several, a number of | much, a little, a great deal of, an amount of |
| Verb agreement | singular or plural                | always singular                               |

**Common uncountable nouns (frequent exam traps):** information, advice, news, furniture, luggage, equipment, homework, knowledge, progress, traffic, weather, bread, money, work, research.

- _She gave me **an** advice._ → _She gave me **some** advice._ / _a piece of advice._
- To count uncountables, use a unit: _a piece of information, a loaf of bread, a bottle of water, an item of news._

**Dual-use nouns** — countable and uncountable with different meanings:

- _a coffee_ (a cup) vs _coffee_ (the substance)
- _a paper_ (a newspaper/report) vs _paper_ (the material)
- _an experience_ (an event) vs _experience_ (know-how)
- _a light_ (a lamp) vs _light_ (illumination)

## Possessive Forms

### 's — Apostrophe s

Used mainly for **people, animals, and time/distance expressions**:

- _John's car, my mother's house, the cat's tail._
- Plural nouns ending in -s take only an apostrophe: _the students' books._
- Time/distance: _a day's work, two weeks' holiday, a mile's walk._

### Of — the "of" structure

Used mainly for **inanimate things**:

- _the door of the room, the end of the film, the leg of the table._
- Long or complex possessors: _the opinions of the people who attended._

### Double genitive

_a friend of mine, a colleague of my father's_ — when the possessor already has a possessive pronoun or 's.

## Compound Nouns

Two or more words forming one noun:

- **Noun + noun:** toothpaste, bedroom, railway station.
- **Adjective + noun:** blackboard, software, greenhouse.
- **Verb-ing + noun:** swimming pool, washing machine.
- **Noun + verb-ing:** handwriting, window-shopping.
- Plural usually falls on the last word: _boyfriends, grown-ups_ — but on the main noun in some compounds: _mothers-in-law, passers-by._

## Notes

- Uncountable nouns take singular verbs and no indefinite article — the most common source of agreement errors (see [Subject Verb Agreement](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Subject Verb Agreement)).
- Hair, fruit, and money are uncountable in the general sense but may be countable when meaning individual items.
