# Subject Verb Agreement

The verb must agree with its subject in **number**: singular subject → singular verb, plural subject → plural verb. For plural formation itself, see [Singular And Plural](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Singular And Plural).

## Basic Rule

- _He works hard._ · _They work hard._
- Intervening phrases do not change agreement — find the real subject: _The quality of the products **is** good._ (not "are")

## Singular Verb Cases

- **Indefinite pronouns:** each, every, everyone, everybody, everything, someone, somebody, anyone, anybody, nobody, no one, either, neither, one, much, little.
  - _Everybody is here._ (Mọi người đều có mặt.)
  - _Neither answer is correct._
- **each/every + noun:** _Each student has a locker._ · _Every boy and girl was present._
- **a number of...** → plural, but **the number of...** → singular:
  - _A number of students **were** absent._ · _The number of students **is** forty._
- **Subjects that look plural but are singular:** news, physics, mathematics, economics, measles; titles of books/films; amounts of time/money/distance seen as one unit.
  - _The news is bad._ · _Ten years is a long time._ · _Five thousand dollars is too much._
- **Gerunds, clauses, and noun clauses as subject:** _Swimming is fun._ · _What he said is true._
- **There + be:** agrees with the noun after it: _There is a book on the table._ · _There are two books on the table._ — see [There Be And It](/English/Grammar/Concepts/There Be And It).

## Plural Verb Cases

- **Both ... and:** _Both Tom and Mary are students._
- **Plural-looking pair nouns:** scissors, trousers, glasses, jeans — _My glasses are new._ (but _a pair of glasses **is** new_).
- **People/police/cattle** are always plural: _The police are investigating._

## Proximity Rule — Verb Agrees with the Nearer Subject

With **or, either ... or, neither ... nor, not only ... but also**, the verb agrees with the subject **closest** to it:

- _Either the teacher or the students **are** wrong._
- _Neither the students nor the teacher **is** wrong._

## Collective Nouns

Family, team, group, class, committee, audience, government —

- **Singular** when the group acts as one unit: _The team is winning._
- **Plural** when members act individually: _The team are arguing among themselves._

## Special Patterns

- **one of + plural noun + singular verb:** _One of my friends **is** a doctor._
- **one of + plural noun + who/that + plural verb** (relative clause describes the whole group): _She is one of the students who **are** always late._
- **the only one of + plural noun + who/that + singular verb:** _He is the only one of the students who **is** never late._
- **Fractions/percentages:** agree with the noun after "of": _Half of the cake **is** gone._ · _Half of the students **are** absent._ · _Fifty percent of the work **is** done._
- **all/most/some + of:** same rule — _Most of the money **is** spent._ · _Most of the tickets **were** sold._
- **many a + singular noun:** formal, singular verb: _Many a student **has** failed this test._
- **more than one + singular noun:** _More than one person **was** hurt._

## Notes

- In questions and with "there be", the subject follows the verb — look ahead to agree correctly.
- Uncountable nouns are always singular — see [Nouns](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Nouns).
- Determiners like each/every/either/neither are covered in [Quantifiers](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Quantifiers).
