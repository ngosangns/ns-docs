# Causative

The causative expresses that the subject **arranges for someone else to do something**, rather than doing it personally.

## Active Causative — Have and Get a Person to Act

| Structure                        | Meaning / tone                 | Example                                                        |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| S + have + person + V (bare)     | arrange/instruct someone to do | _I had the mechanic check the car._ (Tôi bảo thợ kiểm tra xe.) |
| S + get + person + to-infinitive | persuade/arrange someone to do | _I got my brother to help me._ (Tôi nhờ anh trai giúp mình.)   |
| S + make + person + V (bare)     | force/cause someone to do      | _The teacher made us rewrite the essay._                       |

## Passive Causative — Have/Get Something Done

The focus is on the **thing acted upon**; the doer is unimportant or unknown.

**Formula:** S + have/get + object + V3/ed

- _I have my hair cut every month._ (Tôi cắt tóc mỗi tháng — thợ cắt, không phải tôi tự cắt.)
- _She got her laptop repaired._ (Cô ấy đã đem laptop đi sửa.)
- _We are having the house painted._ (Chúng tôi đang thuê người sơn nhà.)

"Get" is more informal than "have"; both work in any tense — conjugate have/get, keep the past participle.

## Special Uses

- **Experiencing something (usually bad):** have + object + V3 where the subject suffers the event, not arranges it.
  - _She had her phone stolen._ (Điện thoại của cô ấy bị mất trộm.)
  - _He got his nose broken in a fight._
- **Ongoing or completed state:** have + object + V-ing / V3.
  - _He had us laughing all evening._ (Anh ấy làm chúng tôi cười suốt buổi tối.)
  - _I'll have the report ready by Friday._ (Tôi sẽ để báo cáo sẵn sàng trước thứ Sáu.)

## Notes

- Active causative pattern difference: **have + person + bare infinitive**, but **get + person + to-infinitive** — a classic exam trap.
- Do not confuse with ordinary "have + object" possession: _I have a car_ vs _I have my car serviced._
