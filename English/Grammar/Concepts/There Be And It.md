# There Be And It

English uses two "dummy" subjects when no real subject exists or when it is better placed later: **there** and **it**.

## There + Be — Existence

"There + be" introduces something that exists or happens — the real subject comes **after** the verb, and the verb agrees with it.

| Tense            | Formula                       | Example                                                      |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Present          | there is/are                  | _There is a book on the desk._ (Có một quyển sách trên bàn.) |
| Past             | there was/were                | _There were many people at the party._                       |
| Present perfect  | there has/have been           | _There have been three accidents this week._                 |
| Future           | there will be                 | _There will be a meeting tomorrow._                          |
| With modals      | there + modal + be            | _There must be a mistake._ · _There may be a delay._         |
| With seem/appear | there seem(s)/appear(s) to be | _There seems to be a problem._                               |

- Questions invert: _Is there any milk?_ · _How many students are there?_
- Tag: _There is a problem, isn't there?_ — see [Questions And Question Tags](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Questions And Question Tags).
- Extended patterns: _There is no point in V-ing_ · _There is no need to V_ · _There is nothing/something + to V_ — see Model 29 of [Sentence Transformation Patterns](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Sentence Transformation Patterns).

## It as an Empty Subject

"It" fills the subject slot for **time, weather, distance, temperature, and situations**:

- _It is 9 o'clock._ (Bây giờ là 9 giờ.) · _It is Monday._
- _It is raining._ · _It is sunny._
- _It is 5 km from here to the station._
- _It is very noisy in here._

## Anticipatory It — It + be + Adj/N + Clause/Infinitive

"It" stands in for a clause placed later, keeping the sentence balanced:

- **It + be + adjective + to-infinitive:** _It is difficult to learn English._ (Học tiếng Anh thì khó.)
- **It + be + adjective + that-clause:** _It is strange that he left early._ — mandative adjectives take the subjunctive: _It is important that she **be** on time_ — see [Subjunctive Mood](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Subjunctive Mood).
- **It + verb + object + to-infinitive:** _It takes me an hour to get to work._ (Tôi mất một tiếng để đến chỗ làm.) — Model 13 of [Sentence Transformation Patterns](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Sentence Transformation Patterns).
- **It + be + time + since/for:** _It is five years since we met._ — Models 9 and 11.
- **Reporting:** _It is said that he is rich._ — impersonal passive, see [Passive Voice](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Passive Voice).
- **find/think/make + it + adj + to-inf/that:** _I find it hard to believe him._ — Model 6.

## Notes

- Do not confuse the dummy "it" with a pronoun: in _It is raining_, "it" refers to nothing — Vietnamese has no equivalent subject.
- There + be shows existence/location; possessive "have" shows ownership: _There is a car outside_ (a car exists there) vs _I have a car_ (I own one).
- "It's" = it is; the possessive is "its" — see [Pronouns](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Pronouns).
