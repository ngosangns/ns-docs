# Discourse Markers

Discourse markers are small words and phrases that organize talk — showing attitude, changing topics, buying time. They carry little meaning themselves but make speech sound natural and connected. Written connectors are covered in [Conjunctions And Connectives](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Conjunctions And Connectives); this note focuses on the **spoken** set.

## Buying Time / Softening

- **well** — pausing before answering, often a partial disagreement: _"Do you like it?" — "Well, it's OK."_
- **you know** — assuming shared knowledge: _I was tired, you know?_
- **I mean** — clarifying or correcting: _He's my brother — I mean, my cousin._
- **let me see / let me think** — buying time: _Let me see... it's about 300 km._
- **sort of / kind of** — hedging: _It's sort of expensive._
- **like** (informal, youth speech) — quoting/approximating: _It was, like, midnight._

## Managing the Conversation

- **actually** — correcting or adding surprising info: _Actually, I've never been there._
- **by the way / incidentally** — introducing a new, related topic: _By the way, did you call Mom?_
- **speaking of / talking of** — picking up on a word just used: _Speaking of exams, mine starts Monday._
- **anyway** — returning to the main topic or closing: _Anyway, back to the plan._
- **as I was saying** — resuming after interruption.
- **that reminds me** — triggered association: _That reminds me, I owe you money._

## Attitude and Contrast in Speech

- **mind you** — qualifying what was just said: _It's cheap. Mind you, it breaks easily._
- **still / having said that** — conceding then contrasting: _The job is hard. Still, I love it._
- **admittedly** — conceding a point: _Admittedly, I was wrong._
- **to be honest / frankly** — flagging honesty: _To be honest, I didn't enjoy it._
- **basically / in a nutshell** — simplifying: _Basically, we need more time._

## Structuring Longer Speech

- **first of all / on top of that / last but not least**
- **in other words / what I mean is** — rephrasing
- **for one thing ... for another** — listing reasons
- **the thing is** — introducing the core problem: _The thing is, we can't afford it._

## Written Counterparts

Speaking markers have formal writing equivalents — don't mix them:

| Spoken              | Written                        |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| well, ...           | (pause; restructure instead)   |
| anyway / by the way | incidentally / parenthetically |
| the thing is        | the main issue is              |
| in a nutshell       | in summary                     |
| stuff like that     | and so on / et cetera          |

## Notes

- A few markers signal fluency; a string of "like, you know, basically" signals the opposite — use one per thought, not per word.
- In exams, markers buy thinking time naturally in speaking parts; in writing, use the formal equivalents.
