# British And American English

British (BrE) and American (AmE) English are mutually understandable — the differences are predictable and fall into four buckets: spelling, vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation.

## Spelling

| Pattern          | BrE                            | AmE                                                   |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| -our / -or       | colour, honour, favour         | color, honor, favor                                   |
| -re / -er        | centre, theatre, litre         | center, theater, liter                                |
| -ise / -ize      | organise, realise              | organize, realize (Oxford spelling also accepts -ize) |
| double -l        | travelled, cancelled           | traveled, canceled                                    |
| -ogue / -og      | catalogue, dialogue            | catalog, dialog                                       |
| -ence / -ense    | defence, licence (n.)          | defense, license                                      |
| ae/oe → e        | anaemia, encyclopaedia, foetus | anemia, encyclopedia, fetus                           |
| -t / -ed past    | learnt, dreamt, burnt, smelt   | learned, dreamed, burned, smelled                     |
| single consonant | fulfil, enrol                  | fulfill, enroll                                       |

## Grammar

- **Present perfect vs past simple:** BrE prefers the perfect for recent events — _I've just eaten_ vs AmE _I just ate._
- **have vs have got:** BrE _Have you got a pen?_ vs AmE _Do you have a pen?_
- **got vs gotten:** AmE uses _gotten_ as the past participle (BrE: got).
- **Collective nouns:** BrE allows plural — _The team are playing well_ — AmE treats them as singular.
- **Prepositions:** _at the weekend_ (BrE) / _on the weekend_ (AmE); _in a team_ / _on a team_; _different to_ / _different than_ (both accept _different from_).
- **Shall:** more alive in BrE suggestions (_Shall we go?_); AmE prefers _should_.
- **Mandative subjunctive:** more common in AmE — see [Subjunctive Mood](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Subjunctive Mood).

## Pronunciation

- **Rhotic r:** AmE pronounces final/pre-consonant r (car, hard); BrE doesn't unless a vowel follows.
- **/æ/ vs /ɑː/:** BrE says _dance, path, can't_ with /ɑː/; AmE with /æ/.
- **/ɒ/ vs /ɑː/:** AmE _hot, stop_ uses /ɑː/; BrE /ɒ/.
- **Flapped t:** AmE _water, better_ sound like /d/ ("wader").

## Notes

- **Consistency rule:** pick one variety per piece of writing — "the colour of my pants" mixes systems (colour = BrE spelling; pants = trousers in BrE means underwear!).
- Exams (THPT, IELTS) accept either variety but penalize mixing.
- Vietnamese textbooks lean British; US media and tech vocabulary lean American — exposure to both is normal.
