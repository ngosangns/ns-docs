# Sentence Stress And Intonation

English is a **stress-timed** language: rhythm comes from stressed syllables, not from every syllable equally — unlike Vietnamese, which is syllable-timed. This is the biggest difference to master for natural-sounding speech.

## Sentence Stress — Which Words Get Stress

- **Stressed (content words):** nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs, question words, negatives.
- **Unstressed (function words):** articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, pronouns, conjunctions.

_I **WANT** to **GO** to the **MAR**ket with **YOU**._

Contrastive stress shifts meaning: _**I** didn't take it_ (someone else did) vs _I didn't **take** it_ (I only borrowed it).

## Weak Forms

Function words shrink to a weak form with schwa /ə/ in normal speech:

| Strong  | Weak  | Example          |
| ------- | ----- | ---------------- |
| a       | /ə/   | a book /əˈbʊk/   |
| and     | /ən/  | bread and butter |
| of      | /əv/  | a cup of tea     |
| to      | /tə/  | go to school     |
| for     | /fə/  | this is for you  |
| can     | /kən/ | I can swim       |
| do/does | /də/  | What do you do?  |

Weak forms are why fast English sounds "mumbly" to Vietnamese ears — train your ear to hear them, don't pronounce them strongly.

## Linking and Connected Speech

- **Consonant → vowel:** sounds merge — _an apple_ → "a-napple", _pick it up_ → "pi-ki-tup".
- **Vowel → vowel:** a glide appears — _go out_ → "go-wout", _I am_ → "I-yam".
- **Intrusive /r/ (BrE):** _law and order_ → "law-rand order", _idea of_ → "idear of".
- **Same consonants merge:** _good day_ → "gooday", _big game_ → "bigame".
- **Common contractions:** I'm, you're, don't, can't, would've, it's; informal gonna (going to), wanna (want to), gotta (got to).

## Intonation — The Melody

| Pattern                     | Used for                                           | Example                                     |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Falling ↘                   | statements, wh-questions, commands, finished lists | _He lives in HANoi._ _Where are you GOing?_ |
| Rising ↗                    | yes/no questions, politeness, surprise             | _Are you READY?_                            |
| Fall-rise ↘↗                | uncertainty, hesitation, polite correction         | _I THINK so..._                             |
| Rise on each + fall on last | lists                                              | _We bought ↗eggs, ↗milk, and ↘bread._       |

**Tag questions:** falling = expecting agreement (_Nice day, ISn't it? ↘_); rising = genuinely asking (_You're coming, ↗aren't you?_) — see [Questions And Question Tags](/English/Grammar/Concepts/Questions And Question Tags).

## Notes

- Don't stress every word equally — that's the single clearest sign of Vietnamese-accented English.
- Practice method: mark the stressed words in a sentence, then hum the sentence's melody before saying the words.
- Intonation carries attitude: a flat _"Really."_ vs a rising _"Really?"_ express doubt vs interest.
