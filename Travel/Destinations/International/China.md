## Self-Guided vs Tour

If you speak some Chinese, self-guided wins - you travel on your own terms and experience the country like a local. Without the language it is still doable but harder: nearly all signage is in Chinese, English speakers are rare, and asking directions gets difficult. In that case a tour is the more comfortable option, or at least travel with someone who knows the place. Some also argue self-guided trips are the best way to truly gauge a travel partner's character and general competence.

## Essential Apps

- **Alipay:** the all-in-one - payments, train tickets, Didi. Link a debit card for spending control, or verify your account (the blue tick) and top up a balance.
- **WeChat:** essential for contacting hotels - many offer free airport pickup if you ask. Also works as a login backbone linking your accounts across Chinese apps (or sign in via Alipay). Without a Chinese phone number, WeChat is your lifeline.
- **12306:** the official train-ticket app/site. Register, complete KYC to the verified tick, then buy tickets and just show your passport at the gate - staff scan it and you walk in.
- **Amap / Baidu Map:** the local navigation apps.
- **Apple Maps (iPhone):** on a Chinese network it automatically switches to Chinese map data - far more complete than Google Maps there, though it can display phantom lines.
- **Google Maps:** poor data inside China.
- **Google Translate:** the default translation tool (works over a 4G travel SIM).
- **DiDi:** the local Grab.
- **Meituan:** find restaurants and buy vouchers for discounts.
- **Dianping:** restaurant reviews. Tip: do **not** register an account - signing up limits results. Just search the dish, filter by rating and location. It shows estimated per-person cost fairly accurately; anything rated 4.0+ is generally safe.
- **Xiaohongshu (RED):** see what young Chinese are doing and where - nearby check-in spots when you have no plan.
- **Trip.com / Ctrip:** hotels, attraction tickets, and some transport. For attraction tickets you must use Trip (Ctrip demands a Chinese ID). Set currency to CNY, pay via Alipay, then just bring your passport to the gate. Trip has fewer coach options than Ctrip, so prices are less competitive on that leg.

## Payments and Money

- Alipay is all you need day to day - treat it like Momo/ZaloPay. Many travelers spend 15 days without touching cash.
- Paying through a linked card incurs a ~3% fee on transactions of 200+ RMB.
- Buying RMB to top up is hit-or-miss - dirty money can get your app frozen. Safer options: pay via card for simplicity (rate differences are small), buy from established sellers, or use Binance P2P for better rates.
- Verify your account before topping up; single transactions may be capped around 500 RMB (adjustable).
- WeChat Pay is what locals use, but is harder for foreigners to set up.
- Credit cards are nearly useless except possibly UnionPay.
- Carry tissues - most public toilets, including nice ones, provide no paper.

## Connectivity

- China Unicom 5G tourist SIM: data-only (no calls), ~320k VND, 30 days, 15 GB. Because it roams from Hong Kong, Facebook/Zalo/Gmail work normally with no VPN.
- Otherwise install a VPN before arrival to use Google services, Facebook, and Google Maps.

## Accommodation

- Trip.com prices beat Agoda and Western apps - one traveler saved ~2 million VND on 7 nights. Downside: Trip requires prepayment.
- Pick a hotel near a metro station - it saves a lot on getting around.
- Book hotels/homestays that advertise English support and western-style toilets if squat toilets bother you.
- **Critical tip:** after booking, print the hotel address **in Chinese characters** - a pinyin or English address like "No.1 Chunxiu Road, Chaoyang, 100027 Beijing" is nearly useless to taxi drivers, while 北京东直门智选假日酒店 works instantly. Also write down the hotel's phone number so drivers can call.

## Cost Breakdown

Costs vary wildly, but a few buckets dominate:

1. **Transport:** flights and high-speed rail are the two big line items. If you position lodging and sightseeing near stations, taxi costs stay small; metro fares are pocket change. Buses are cheap too, but impractical if you cannot read the stops.
2. **Food:** splurging on big restaurants and specialties adds up, but everyday eating is cheap - breakfast runs about 10-20 RMB for noodles or bao + drink.
3. **Lodging:** varies hugely by star rating and season. Dorm beds are the cheap option and good for meeting other travelers. Warning: peak-season prices can multiply tenfold, not merely double or triple like in Vietnam.
4. **Attraction tickets:** depends on the sights, but it is a real budget line.

Traveling in a group of 3-4 is the sweet spot: shared taxis, shared tables, shared rooms. One experienced traveler consistently spends under 10 million VND/person for roughly a week (low season, mixing flights and trains) while eating very well.

## Packing for Cold Weather

- Ear covers - critical, your ears can go numb without warning.
- Scarf/buff to cover nose and cheeks, plus cheap anti-chap balm.
- Layers: cotton base that absorbs sweat, thin wool, and an outer jacket - preferably water-resistant in case melting snow soaks through.
- Thin thermal leggings plus khaki/jeans, with an outer knee wrap.
- 27 cm socks past the calf; tall waterproof boots (Timberland-style). Alternatively buy cheap but decent boots at the underground markets there.
- Hair conditioner against dryness.
- Touchscreen gloves or a stylus - bare hands go red within minutes.
- Power bank: Androids last all day; iPhones can shut down in the cold.
- About 7 cotton tees for 7 days; the layers underneath do not need daily changing.

## Other Tips

- **Transit fare hack ("skiplagging"):** booking a cheaper connecting itinerary and skipping the final leg sometimes works, but depends on the airline - do not check luggage and do not buy round-trip if you try it. Chinese connecting fares are usually only cheap when kept on the same airline.
- **Finding local guides/interpreters:** hang around Vietnamese student and expat groups in China - members often guide tours or interpret. English-facing Chinese tour sites are another avenue.
- For older travelers or a gentler trip, Phượng Hoàng Cổ Trấn is cheaper and easier going.
