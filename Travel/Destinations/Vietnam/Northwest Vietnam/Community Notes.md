# Northwest Vietnam - Community Notes

Distilled field knowledge from touring community threads (mainly VOZ), covering the wider Northwest beyond Hà Giang. For the Hà Giang loop itself, see [Ha Giang](/Travel/Destinations/Vietnam/Ha Giang).

## Geography and Riding Character

Geographically, "Tây Bắc" means the land west of the Hoàng Liên range and north of the Mã river (the Đà river basin): Lai Châu, Điện Biên, Sơn La, Hòa Bình. Loosely, it can extend to the right bank of the Red River, adding Lào Cai, Yên Bái, and Phú Thọ.

- Northwest terrain is a repeating pattern: climb a pass, drop into a wide valley. Northeast terrain is the opposite - lower hills, small valleys along big rivers - so roads twist more. Riding the main highways, all the Northeast provinces feel similarly easy.
- The most beautiful and most rideable belt runs along the Hoàng Liên range: **Yên Bái - Lai Châu - Lào Cai**.
- Community consensus: Yên Bái and Hà Giang are the two most beautiful provinces; Điện Biên and Lai Châu are scenic but a step below. Lai Châu's Mường Tè is the most remote corner - raw and primal.
- Lào Cai is a standout: Sapa needs no introduction; Y Tý has vast primary forests; Bắc Hà has festivals that run through the night; even Tằng Loỏng's factory haze mixed with mist has its own strange charm.
- One rider claims "Yên Bái has now outclassed Đà Lạt"; another praised spring trips through Mai Châu and Mộc Châu - less crowded than Hà Giang, still pristine, locals cheerful.
- If you reach Lào Cai, you can bring a passport and pop across the Chinese border gate for a look.

## The Four Great Passes and Other Roads

- **Mã Pì Lèng (Hà Giang):** abyss on one side, mountain wall on the other - majestic, gorgeous, and easy to ride.
- **Khau Phạ (Yên Bái):** ~30 km; approach through Tú Lệ past villages and terraces - stunning in golden season.
- **Ô Quy Hồ (Lào Cai - Lai Châu):** ~50 km; descending toward Lào Cai is thrilling, grand scenery, demands a firmer hand.
- **Pha Đin (Điện Biên):** the one many riders still have not had the chance to try.

Other roads the community rates:

- **Trạm Tấu - Thanh Sơn - Thu Cúc (Yên Bái):** for pure riding pleasure - continuous smooth curves on good surface - some prefer it even to the famous passes.
- **Km32 Tân Nguyên (Yên Bình) - Mậu A (Văn Yên):** a rarely mentioned but genuinely beautiful pass.

## Suggested Loops

- **Starter tour:** Hanoi -> Thanh Sơn (bike) / Yên Bái (car) -> Nghĩa Lộ -> Tú Lệ -> Khau Phạ pass -> Mù Cang Chải.
- **Harder version:** Hanoi -> Tà Xùa -> Trạm Tấu -> Nghĩa Lộ.
- **Full Northwest + Hà Giang:** Hanoi -> Hòa Bình -> Mộc Châu -> Phà Vạn Yên -> Tà Xùa -> Trạm Tấu -> Nghĩa Lộ -> Tú Lệ -> Khau Phạ -> Mù Cang Chải -> Than Uyên -> Ô Quy Hồ -> Sapa -> Y Tý -> Lào Cai -> Bắc Hà -> Cốc Pài town -> Hoàng Su Phì (Bản Phùng) -> Hà Giang city -> Quản Bạ -> Phó Bảng -> Lũng Cú -> Đồng Văn -> Mèo Vạc.
- **Big traverse into Hà Giang:** Tà Xùa -> Trạm Tấu -> Nghĩa Lộ -> Tú Lệ -> Mù Cang Chải -> Tam Đường -> Ô Quy Hồ -> Sapa -> Lào Cai -> Bắc Hà -> Xín Mần -> Hoàng Su Phì -> Vị Xuyên -> Hà Giang -> Yên Minh -> Lũng Cú -> Đồng Văn -> Mèo Vạc -> Mậu Duệ -> Du Già -> Hà Giang -> Tuyên Quang -> Hanoi. If you have the days, do a full Northwest loop before entering Hà Giang - a detour just for Tà Xùa wastes the ride.
- **Suggested progression for new riders:** Trạm Tấu (Yên Bái) first, then Tà Xùa, Y Tý, Tà Chì Nhù, Hà Giang; once hardened, run a full loop like Hanoi - Mộc Châu - Điện Biên - Mù Cang Chải - Yên Bái - Hanoi (about 3 days, 1,000+ km).
- **Return option from the far north:** Mèo Vạc -> Cao Bằng -> Bắc Kạn -> Thái Nguyên -> Hanoi - scenic but grueling.

## Riding Notes

- **Realistic daily distance:** fit riders average about 350 km and ~8 hours in the saddle per day, stopping wherever the scenery demands. One group's only mishap in 3 days was a deep-cornering crash that ended in a clinic visit - pace yourself.
- **Machines:** a manual-clutch bike of ~150 cc+ and a steady hand are enough for 60-70 km/h on mountain roads. For people unused to long mountain riding, 350 km/day is still hard.
- **Winter riding:** brutally cold on a motorbike - plan accordingly.
- **Truck traffic:** the Hanoi - Hà Giang/Cao Bằng highway is crowded with trucks and long container rigs. Bigger bikes pass safely and quickly; small bikes end up crawling behind a rig for kilometers - uncomfortable and risky.

## Golden-Season Rice Calendar

Updated community intel on when the terraces ripen, for trip planning:

- **Nậm Cang:** July 20 - 30. Rice fully ripe; motorbike access is not too hard, and the deeper you ride into the valleys the better it gets.
- **Hoàng Su Phì (Hà Giang):** September 15 - October 5. If any single route is lined with terraces end to end, it is this one. Suggested line: Bắc Hà - Suôi Thầu - Bản Phùng - Nậm Khoà mâm xôi fields, combined with Chiêu Lầu Thi cloud hunting. Great for offroad fans, but the road is rough - be fit.
- **Mù Cang Chải (Yên Bái):** September 5 - 20. Arguably the most beautiful in Vietnam - even Westerners fly in for it. Roads are now much better than before, sights are close together, and entry fees run just 10,000 - 20,000 VND.
- **Tả Van (Sapa):** August 25 - September 10. The easiest option - right next to Sapa with the fullest services; hike between villages, sip coffee over the rice.
- **Y Tý (Lào Cai):** August 25 - September 15. Recently famous with young riders via the legendary "S-curve" road. Open and airy, but the Sapa - Y Tý road is still rough - first-timers should ride with an experienced lead.
- **Tà Xùa (Sơn La):** August 25 - September 10. Cloud hunting plus sky-high terraces; deep inside villages like Xím Vàng you can find mâm-xôi fields rivaling Hoàng Su Phì or Mù Cang Chải.
- **Trùng Khánh (Cao Bằng):** August 25 - September 10. Follow the veteran photographers: Phong Nậm - Ngọc Côn valley produces lifetime shots. What Bắc Sơn is to Lạng Sơn and Mù Cang Chải is to Yên Bái, Trùng Khánh is to Cao Bằng.

Dates are approximate but proven close in practice.

## Mù Cang Chải, Up Close

One rider's take: Mù Cang Chải is among the very few Northwest places not yet heavily commercialized - truly wild scenery, golden terraces flickering through dark, deep pine forests, and air so clean it relaxes the body. Through Tú Lệ the smell of cốm carries for a kilometer. Eat and sleep inside the villages - cheap, run entirely by local people. Look for "khảu lang", a dish somewhere between cốm and xôi that locals swear by.
